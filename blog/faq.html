Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about learning Egyptian Arabic (Masri) with SpeakMasri - for partners, in-laws, and family.

What is SpeakMasri?

SpeakMasri is an app that teaches real spoken Egyptian Arabic (Masri) - the way people actually talk at home with family and friends, not the formal textbook language you learned in school.

Instead of drilling you on abstract grammar or newsreader phrases, SpeakMasri drops you into everyday situations with your partner, in-laws, and friends, so you can:

  • understand what's being said around you
  • answer in a way that feels natural, not robotic
  • slowly start to "belong" in the family WhatsApp groups and living-room conversations

What is Egyptian Arabic / Masri? How is it different from Modern Standard Arabic (MSA)?

Egyptian Arabic (Masri) is the everyday dialect most Egyptians actually speak at home, on the street, and online. Modern Standard Arabic (MSA) is the formal, standardized language you'll find in textbooks, news broadcasts, and exams.

They're related, but not interchangeable:

  • MSA is great for reading and formal contexts.
  • Masri is what your partner's family is using at the dinner table.

SpeakMasri focuses on Masri, not MSA.

For a deeper dive into Masri vs MSA (with real examples), check out the existing article on the site: "What is Egyptian Arabic?" - this FAQ just gives the short version.

Who is SpeakMasri for?

SpeakMasri is designed for people who care less about passing an exam and more about feeling at home with Egyptians they love. Most learners fall into one of these groups:

  • Partners of Egyptians - you have an Egyptian boyfriend/girlfriend, fiancé(e), husband or wife and want to understand and speak the language their family actually uses.
  • In-laws and extended family - you're marrying into, or already part of, an Egyptian family and want to catch the jokes, questions, and little comments around you.
  • Upcoming trips to Egypt - you're visiting Egypt and want to sound more like "someone who belongs" than a tourist with a phrasebook.
  • Heritage / reconnection - you have Egyptian roots and want to reconnect with the way your family speaks at home.

If any of those sound like you, you're the person SpeakMasri is built for.

How long are the lessons?

SpeakMasri lessons are short on purpose:

  • Each core lesson is around 3 minutes.
  • They're designed so you can finish one on the sofa, on the commute, or while waiting for your partner to get ready.

You don't need an hour and a workbook to make progress - just a few focused minutes at a time.

What kind of phrases will I learn?

SpeakMasri is built around real relationship and family language, not random travel phrases.

You'll move through situations like:

  • being welcomed into the family home
  • answering small-talk questions from in-laws
  • checking in on your partner after a long day
  • handling basic everyday logistics (food, rides, plans) in Masri

The phrases are organised by register - for example:

  • Formal - respectful phrases you'd use with elders or in-laws
  • Casual - everyday language with friends, siblings, peers
  • Intimate - things you'd say only to your partner

That way, you can choose what feels right for the situation and relationship.

Can my Egyptian partner or family help customise my phrases?

Yes. SpeakMasri includes a feature that lets your partner or family help you make the app feel like your life.

They can:

  • add real family names (so you're not just learning "my mother-in-law" in the abstract)
  • include in-jokes and nicknames
  • suggest phrases they actually use with you or in the family group chats

You'll then see those personalised details appear inside relevant lessons, so what you're practising matches how your people really talk.

SpeakMasri currently supports several base languages for explanations and UI, including:

That means you can learn Masri with explanations in the language you're most comfortable reading.

Do I need any Arabic background to start?

No prior Arabic is required.

SpeakMasri is designed for complete beginners who may not read Arabic script yet. You'll see Masri written in ways that are easy to sound out (Franco-Arabic transliteration), and you can build from there at your own pace.

If you do have some background in MSA or another dialect, you'll still find that the focus on real, spoken Masri at home fills in gaps that textbooks don't cover.
