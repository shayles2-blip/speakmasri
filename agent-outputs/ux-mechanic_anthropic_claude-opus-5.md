## 1. Swipe cards here: no

Not because swipe is bad — because it's the wrong verb for this content and this person.

- **Swipe encodes a self-judgment, not a retrieval.** "I know this" is the fluency-heuristic trap: your user recognises *ana baḥebbik* on a card, swipes right, then freezes in the kitchen when their mother-in-law speaks first. Recognition-only mechanics inflate confidence precisely where this app's stakes punish it.
- **Your card is too dense to swipe.** Audio play button + Arabic script + franco + register tag + literal gloss = a card that needs 3–4 taps to fully consume. Horizontal swipe competes with those taps and with RTL text scanning. Users will mis-fire constantly on a 480px thumb arc.
- **Swipe decks pay off on volume + idle time** (200 kanji on a bus). Your deck is ~12 phrases per scenario, consumed *deliberately* before a dinner. There's no boredom to absorb.

Keep the underlying spaced-repetition scheduler — just drive it with a 3-button tap rating **after** production, never a pre-emptive swipe.

## 2. Better mechanic: the "Turn-Taker" — branching micro-dialogue

Your users' real failure isn't vocabulary, it's *choosing the wrong register with the wrong person, in real time, under social pressure.* Nothing in your current four exercise types trains turn-taking or register selection under time.

**Interaction (480px):** Top: a persona chip — *Mama Nadia, 62, wife's mother*. Her line auto-plays as an audio bubble with a waveform; tap to replay, long-press for franco/script. Bottom: three reply cards, each visibly register-tagged (intimate / casual / formal). Pick one → **you must say it aloud** (hold-to-record, waveform confirms voice detected; "I said it" fallback if mic is denied) → her next line plays, and it *changes based on your register*: formal to your mother-in-law gets warmth; casual gets a slightly cooler reply. A small warmth meter, not a score. 3–4 turns, ~60 seconds. Rewind one turn, no fail state.

## 3. Where it goes

**New lesson capstone, gating the "say this tonight" mission** — it's the rehearsal before the real thing, so the mission stops being a cold jump. If you need build budget, cut **match-pairs**: it's your weakest exercise, pure recognition, and Turn-Taker subsumes its job.
