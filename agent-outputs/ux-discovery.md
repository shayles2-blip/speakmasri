## 1. The wedge: Partner "Alex" — pick it, kill the other two for v1

**Traveler Sam has the best acquisition and the worst everything else.** Highest search intent ("learn Egyptian Arabic before my trip"), but the need expires. Sam's honest usage pattern is 3–10 sessions in the two weeks before a flight, then permanent churn. Every KPI in your spec (streak > 3 days, D1 retention) is measuring a behavior Sam has no reason to perform. Zero-budget means you need word of mouth; Sam's trip ends and he never mentions you again.

**Heritage Nour is the hardest content problem.** Nour typically has partial passive comprehension and an emotional block about sounding like a foreigner. Beginner MCQ drills insult her and conversational content is out of MVP scope. Wrong first customer for a 4-exercise-type app.

**Alex wins on retention and distribution, which is what a no-brand v1 needs:**
- **Built-in daily trigger that isn't your push notification:** a real Egyptian person in her life, every day.
- **Built-in feedback + validation loop:** the in-laws are free pronunciation QA and the emotional reward you can't manufacture.
- **Findable for $0:** these people self-identify in dense, small communities (r/Egypt, r/AmericanExpatsEgypt, Facebook "married to an Egyptian" groups, TikTok comment sections). You can reach 200 of them by hand this month.
- **Non-expiring motive:** the relationship doesn't end in 10 days.
- **Free referral surface:** the Egyptian partner shares it, in Egypt, for free.

## 2. Rewritten MoSCoW — Alex only

**MUST:** Native-authored content in *exactly* Alex's situations (greetings/politeness with elders, family vocabulary, food/hosting, phone-call phrases, terms of endearment); 3-form display; audio; 2 exercise types only (multiple-choice + fill-in-blank); progress unlocking; local persistence; a "say this to them tonight" real-world mission per lesson.

**SHOULD:** Streak; XP; match-the-pairs; lesson-complete screen; email capture.

**COULD:** Listen & choose; profile page.

**WON'T (v1):** **Leaderboard** (dead surface below ~200 DAU — an empty or 4-person leaderboard actively signals a dead app), **hearts** (see below), auth beyond an email field, accuracy percentages.

## 3. Biggest retention risk: the **trigger** leg doesn't exist

You have no push channel (a web app on a domain has no reliable notification), no social graph, and your only social pull — the leaderboard — is empty at launch. So the hook loop is really Action → Reward → Investment with **nothing** restarting it. Streaks only create loss aversion *if something tells you the streak is at risk*.

Secondary, and cheap to fix: **hearts punish the exact learner whose blocker is fear of embarrassment.** With no gem economy to refill them, hearts only produce forced session-abandonment. Cut them; use "correct the mistake to continue."

Fix the trigger with what you have: capture email/WhatsApp at signup, send one daily 7pm nudge containing the actual phrase of the day.

## 4. Two-week hypotheses

1. **Alex-fit:** Partner-motivated users out-retain trip-motivated ones. Metric: D7 retention split by onboarding "why are you here?" answer. **Pass: partner cohort D7 ≥ 25% AND ≥ 2× traveler cohort.**
2. **Trigger:** External nudge, not intrinsic pull, drives return. Metric: D7 retention, nudge-opted-in vs. not. **Pass: ≥ 15 percentage-point gap.**
3. **Real-world reward beats XP:** Metric: % of lesson-completers who tap "I said it to them ✅". **Pass: ≥ 30% on lesson 1, and those users complete ≥ 2× more lessons in week 1.** Fail: < 15% → your reward is fake and content, not gamification, is the problem.
