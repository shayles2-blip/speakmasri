1) Swipe-based “I know / still learning” review: **No as a core mechanic; only marginal as an optional filter.**  
For SpeakMasri’s use case (prep right before/after real partner conversations), swipe cards optimize for **fast self-assessment**, not **retrieval + pronunciation + register selection under pressure**. Your content needs **audio + Arabic script (RTL) + franco + meaning + register badge** visible at once on a 480px viewport. Cards either get visually cramped or hide crucial cues behind taps, which breaks the “quick, confident use tonight” goal. Also, “I know this” is notoriously unreliable (illusion of competence); what matters here is **can I produce it aloud with the right level of intimacy/formality**. Swipe tends to reward speed and completion, not **production**. It can still work as a lightweight **phrasebook triage** (keep vs revisit), but it won’t outperform targeted exercises/missions for this persona.

2) Stronger complementary mechanic: **“Hold-to-speak rehearsal + partner-ready send”** (production-first).  
Interaction: each phrase becomes a compact rehearsal tile:  
- Tap plays native audio once.  
- Then **press-and-hold to record** yourself; on release, you immediately hear **A/B playback** (native → you), with a simple “Try again” loop.  
- After two attempts, you choose a **register chip** (intimate/casual/formal) and optionally add a **context note** (“say to her mom when greeting”).  
- One-tap **“Send to partner”** exports as audio + text (Arabic+franco) via WhatsApp (or copy/share sheet).  
Rationale: this directly trains **retrieval + pronunciation + contextual encoding** and creates a real-world bridge (social accountability) without gamified streaks.

3) Where it fits: **Add as a new mode tied to the existing “say this tonight” mission.**  
Keep current exercise types for comprehension; then mission unlocks a **Rehearsal Pack** (5–10 phrases) using the hold-to-speak loop. This becomes the default “last mile” before real conversation—more aligned than generic swipe review.
